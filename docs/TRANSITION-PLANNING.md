# Transition planning and conflicts

0.2.0 restarted a fixed note-edge fade toward zero. 3.0 constructs a single common-mode correction plan before rendering. Adjacent regions hold their regional displacement through reliable stable material. The transition interval lies between stable anchors for a connected phrase, or inside a genuinely silent separation where available.

Transitions use the minimum-jerk quintic `10x^3 - 15x^4 + 6x^5`, whose endpoint slope and curvature are zero. It is chosen because its timing/slope can be checked, not because all splines sound natural. For a difference D and duration T, peak slope is `1.875*abs(D)/T`. The initial conservative slope budget is 400 cents/second (an engineering limit to validate, not a universal perceptual threshold). If the available interval cannot accommodate the requested difference, the pair is projected toward a confidence/uncertainty-weighted shared centre and labeled reduced accuracy. The planner does not extend an expressive glide arbitrarily to hide the conflict.

Discrete events keep a constant pitched-interior displacement. No sustain ramp follows harmonic loudness changes. Short-window energy onsets relocate late stable-note boundaries. Protection starts 12 ms before the strongest compatible onset observation: its first 40 ms is dry, followed by a 40 ms smooth dry/processed bridge. The onset estimate is heuristic; tests also check the first 15 ms after the independently known synthesized attacks. This is an explicit compromise between attack identity and tuning from the first cycle. It can produce phase coloration and must pass attack/timing/level tests plus real listening. It is not advertised as perfectly preserving every transient while fully shifting that same transient.

If old/new tail evidence implies an overlap and their desired offsets conflict beyond the requested precision, the entire connected overlap group is declined. A common offset may be shared when compatible. This is a safe whole-signal strategy, not event separation. Detection can miss overlaps when one tail is weak or harmonic with the next note; those are critical listening cases.

Tests sample C at 1 ms, require <=1-cent correction steps, preserve plateau targets when feasible, force an impossible-boundary conflict, inspect actual rendered pitch and preserve original PCM for a declined overlap. The quintic's smoothness is not alone a quality pass. Remaining work: multiregion constrained optimization, perceptually calibrated slope limits, reliable transient boundaries, and real ney/string/arpeggio listening.
